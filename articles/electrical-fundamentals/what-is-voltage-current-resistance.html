Voltage, Current, Resistance & Electrical Power Explained

Understand the four fundamental electrical quantities — voltage, electric current, resistance, and electrical power — with simple explanations, formulas, units, and practical engineering concepts.

By Prasun Barua • Electrical Engineering • Updated August 2026

bolt Quick Overview

Voltage provides the electrical potential difference that can drive current through a circuit. Resistance opposes current flow, while electrical power describes the rate at which electrical energy is transferred or converted.

P = V × I

What Is Voltage?

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points in a circuit. It represents the energy transferred per unit of electric charge between those points.

Voltage is commonly represented by the symbol V and is measured in volts (V).

In a simple electrical circuit, a voltage source such as a battery creates a potential difference that can cause electric current to flow when a complete conducting path is available.

V = W ÷ Q

Where:

  • V = voltage in volts (V)
  • W = energy in joules (J)
  • Q = electric charge in coulombs (C)

What Is Electric Current?

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a conductor or electrical circuit.

Current is represented by the symbol I and is measured in amperes (A).

I = Q ÷ t

Where:

  • I = current in amperes (A)
  • Q = electric charge in coulombs (C)
  • t = time in seconds (s)

In practical electrical engineering, current is one of the most important quantities used when selecting conductors, protective devices, transformers, switchgear, and electrical equipment.

What Is Resistance?

Electrical resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current through a material or circuit component.

Resistance is represented by the symbol R and is measured in ohms (Ω).

R = V ÷ I

According to Ohm's Law, when voltage and resistance are known, the current can be calculated using:

I = V ÷ R

Resistance depends on factors such as the material, conductor length, conductor cross-sectional area, and temperature.

For a uniform conductor, resistance can also be expressed as:

R = ρL ÷ A

Where ρ is the material's resistivity, L is conductor length, and A is the conductor cross-sectional area.

What Is Electrical Power?

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, converted, or consumed by an electrical device.

Power is represented by the symbol P and is measured in watts (W).

P = V × I

For a resistive load, power can also be calculated using the following equivalent forms:

Voltage & Current P = V × I
Using Current P = I² × R
Using Voltage P = V² ÷ R

How Voltage, Current, Resistance & Power Are Related

These electrical quantities are closely related. Ohm's Law provides the fundamental relationship between voltage, current, and resistance, while the electrical power equation connects these quantities to power.

Quantity Symbol Unit Common Formula
Voltage V Volt (V) V = I × R
Current I Ampere (A) I = V ÷ R
Resistance R Ohm (Ω) R = V ÷ I
Power P Watt (W) P = V × I

Simple Electrical Example

Suppose a 24 V DC source is connected to a 12 Ω resistor. The current can be calculated using Ohm's Law.

Step 1 — Known Values

V = 24 V

R = 12 Ω

Step 2 — Apply Ohm's Law

I = V ÷ R

Step 3 — Calculate Current

I = 24 ÷ 12 = 2 A
I = 2 A

Therefore, the current flowing through the resistor is 2 amperes.

Step 4 — Calculate Electrical Power

P = V × I = 24 × 2 = 48 W

The resistor therefore dissipates 48 W of electrical power.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is voltage?

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. It is measured in volts (V).

What is electric current?

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a circuit. It is measured in amperes (A).

What is resistance?

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current. It is measured in ohms (Ω).

What is electrical power?

Electrical power represents the rate at which electrical energy is transferred, converted, or consumed. It is measured in watts (W).

What is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law describes the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in an electrical circuit. It can be written as V = I × R.
