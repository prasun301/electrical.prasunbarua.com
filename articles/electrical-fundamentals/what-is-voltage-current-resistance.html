What is Voltage, Current, and Resistance?

Published on August 13, 2026 • 5 min read

Every electrical system around us—from smartphone chargers to large power grids—operates on three core principles: Voltage, Current, and Resistance. Understanding how these parameters interact is essential for studying electronics and electrical engineering.

1. Voltage (Electrical Potential Difference)

Voltage is the pressure or push that drives electric charges through a conducting wire. It is measured in Volts (V) and represented mathematically by the symbol $V$ or $E$. Without voltage, electric charge remains stationary.

2. Current (Rate of Charge Flow)

Current represents the actual volume of electrical charge flowing past a given point in a circuit per unit of time. It is measured in Amperes (A) or "Amps" and is represented by the symbol $I$.

3. Resistance (Opposition to Flow)

Resistance is the force that opposes the flow of electric current through a material. Components designed specifically to introduce resistance are called resistors. Resistance is measured in Ohms ($\Omega$) and represented by the symbol $R$.

The Water Pipe Analogy

An intuitive way to visualize electric circuits is by imagining water flowing through a pipe:

  • Voltage corresponds to water pressure supplied by a pump or water tower.
  • Current is the rate of flow (e.g., liters of water per second).
  • Resistance is the narrowness or friction of the pipe obstructing the water.

Ohm's Law: Connecting the Triad

Formulated by German physicist Georg Ohm, Ohm's Law defines the proportional relationship between these three fundamental quantities:

Ohm's Law Equation

$$V = I \times R$$

Where $V$ is Voltage in Volts, $I$ is Current in Amperes, and $R$ is Resistance in Ohms.

Key Takeaways

  • Voltage: Potential difference (Pressure).
  • Current: Rate of charge flow (Movement).
  • Resistance: Opposing friction (Restriction).
  • Formula: $V = I \times R$
