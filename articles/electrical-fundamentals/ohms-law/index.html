What Are Voltage, Current & Resistance?

Voltage, Current & Resistance

Almost every electrical circuit can be understood by looking at three fundamental quantities: voltage, current, and resistance.

These quantities describe different aspects of electrical behavior. Voltage represents electrical potential difference, current describes the flow of electric charge, and resistance describes how strongly a material or component opposes that flow.

Their relationship is described by Ohm's Law for circuits and components that exhibit an ohmic relationship.

V = I × R

What Is Voltage?

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points in a circuit. It represents the amount of energy available per unit of electric charge between those points.

Voltage is commonly described as the electrical potential that can cause current to flow when a closed conductive path is available.

Voltage Unit

Voltage is measured in volts (V). A multimeter set to voltage measures the potential difference between two points.

Voltage V = W ÷ Q

where W is energy in joules and Q is electric charge in coulombs.

Common Voltage Examples

  • A single AA battery is typically around 1.5 V.
  • A common USB power supply provides 5 V.
  • A typical household AC supply may be approximately 230 V depending on the electrical system.

What Is Current?

Electric current is the rate at which electric charge flows through a conductor or circuit.

Current is represented by the symbol I and is measured in amperes (A).

Electric Current I = Q ÷ t

where Q is electric charge in coulombs and t is time in seconds.

For example, if 10 coulombs of charge pass through a conductor in 2 seconds, the average current is:

Q = 10 C

t = 2 s

I = Q ÷ t

I = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 A

Current = 5 A

What Is Resistance?

Resistance is the opposition offered by a material or electrical component to the flow of electric current.

Resistance is represented by the symbol R and is measured in ohms (Ω).

A higher resistance generally results in lower current for a given voltage, while a lower resistance generally allows more current to flow.

Resistance R = V ÷ I

Factors Affecting Resistance

The resistance of a conductor depends on several factors, including its material, length, cross-sectional area, and temperature.

Conductor Resistance R = ρL ÷ A

where ρ is resistivity, L is conductor length, and A is cross-sectional area.

Voltage vs Current vs Resistance

The three quantities have different roles in an electrical circuit.

Quantity Symbol Unit Basic Meaning
Voltage V Volt (V) Electrical potential difference
Current I Ampere (A) Rate of electric charge flow
Resistance R Ohm (Ω) Opposition to current flow

How Voltage, Current & Resistance Are Related

Voltage, current, and resistance are closely related in an electrical circuit. For a component following Ohm's Law, changing one quantity affects the others.

V Voltage Voltage increases when current or resistance increases, assuming the other quantity remains constant.
I Current Current increases with voltage and decreases with resistance.
R Resistance Resistance limits the amount of current flowing for a given voltage.

Ohm's Law

The relationship between voltage, current, and resistance is commonly expressed using Ohm's Law:

V = I × R

The equation can be rearranged to calculate any one of the three quantities.

Voltage V = I × R
Current I = V ÷ R
Resistance R = V ÷ I

Worked Example

Consider a simple DC circuit supplied by 24 V with a resistor of 12 Ω. What current flows through the resistor?

Step 1 — Identify the known values

Voltage (V) = 24 V

Resistance (R) = 12 Ω

Step 2 — Choose the formula

I = V ÷ R

Step 3 — Substitute the values

I = 24 ÷ 12
I = 2 A

Therefore, the current flowing through the resistor is 2 amperes.

Engineering Notes

Voltage, current, and resistance are fundamental concepts used throughout electrical engineering, including power systems, electronics, control systems, instrumentation, and renewable energy.

In practical electrical systems, engineers also consider other quantities such as power, energy, impedance, frequency, and power factor.

Electrical Power P = V × I

Combining Ohm's Law with the power equation gives additional useful relationships:

P = I² × R
P = V² ÷ R
Engineering Note:

Ohm's Law describes the voltage-current relationship for ohmic behavior. Not every electrical component behaves as a constant resistance over all operating conditions. Devices such as diodes and semiconductor components can have nonlinear voltage-current characteristics.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is voltage?

Voltage is the electrical potential difference between two points. It is measured in volts (V) and represents the potential available to drive electric charge through a circuit.

What is electric current?

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a circuit. It is measured in amperes (A).

What is resistance?

Resistance is the opposition to electric current flow. It is measured in ohms (Ω).

What is the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance?

For an ohmic relationship, they are related by Ohm's Law: V = I × R.

What happens to current when resistance increases?

If voltage remains constant, increasing resistance causes current to decrease according to I = V ÷ R.

What happens to current when voltage increases?

If resistance remains constant, increasing voltage causes current to increase proportionally according to I = V ÷ R.
