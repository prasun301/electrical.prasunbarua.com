Ohm's Law Explained: Formula, Examples & Calculations

Ohm's Law is one of the most foundational relationships in electrical engineering. It describes how voltage, current, and resistance interact within an electrical circuit.

Quick Answer

Ohm's Law states that the current ($I$) flowing through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage ($V$) across the two points and inversely proportional to the resistance ($R$).

V = I × R

What Is Ohm's Law?

Ohm's Law is a fundamental principle used in electrical and electronic engineering to calculate the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in an electrical circuit.

When the resistance of a circuit is constant, increasing the voltage results in a proportional increase in current. Conversely, if resistance increases for a given voltage, current decreases.
